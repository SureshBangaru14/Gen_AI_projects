01. What is Chunking :

	- Chunking is the process of breaking a large document into smaller, meaningful pieces called chunks.
	- In RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation), documents are usually too large to directly embed and search efficiently. So we split them into smaller sections before creating embeddings.
	
	- Simple example
	
		- Suppose you have a 10-page PDF:
		
		
			10-Page PDF
			     │
			       ▼
			  Chunking
			     │
			     ├── Chunk 1
			     ├── Chunk 2
			     ├── Chunk 3
			     ├── Chunk 4
			     └── ...
			     
		- For example:

			Original Document:


			Employee Leave Policy


			Employees are entitled to 18 days of annual leave.
			Leave requests must be submitted through the HR portal.
			Managers must approve leave requests.
			Employees can carry forward up to 5 unused days.
			
			
			
		- After chunking:

			Chunk 1:
			Employees are entitled to 18 days of annual leave.


			Chunk 2:
			Leave requests must be submitted through the HR portal.


			Chunk 3:
			Managers must approve leave requests.


			Chunk 4:
			Employees can carry forward up to 5 unused days.
			
02. Why Chunking :

	- Chunking is required in RAG because large documents should not be treated as one single piece of text. We divide them into smaller, meaningful pieces so the system can retrieve the most relevant information for a user's question.
	
	
	1. Large Documents Are Too Big :

		Imagine a 500-page HR policy document:

		500-page PDF
		     │
		       ▼
		   Chunking
		     │
		     ├── Chunk 1
		     ├── Chunk 2
		     ├── Chunk 3
		     ├── ...
		     └── Chunk 500+

		Instead of processing the entire document for every question, RAG can search the smaller chunks.
		
		
	2. Better Retrieval Accuracy :

		Suppose the user asks:

		"How many annual leave days are available?"

		Without chunking:

			500-page document
			       ↓
			One embedding
			       ↓
			Poorly focused representation
			
		With chunking:

			Document
			   ↓
			Chunks
			   ↓
			Embedding each chunk
			   ↓
			Vector Database
			   ↓
			Similarity Search
			   ↓
			Annual Leave Chunk

			The system can retrieve the specific chunk containing the answer.
			
	
	3. Better Embeddings :

		An embedding represents the meaning of text.

		Consider:

		Chunk 1 → Leave Policy
		Chunk 2 → Salary Policy
		Chunk 3 → Attendance Policy
		Chunk 4 → Insurance Policy

		Each embedding is focused on a specific topic.

		This is generally better for semantic search than creating one embedding for:

		Leave + Salary + Attendance + Insurance + Travel + ...
		
	
	4. Avoid Context-Window Problems :

		LLMs have a maximum context window.

		For example:

			Large Document
			      ↓
			100,000 tokens

			You don't want to send all 100,000 tokens for every question.

			Instead:

			User Question
			      ↓
			Vector Search
			      ↓
			Top 3–5 Relevant Chunks
			      ↓
			LLM Context
			      ↓
			Answer

			This keeps the prompt manageable.
			
	5. Reduce Token Usage :

		Suppose:

		Entire document = 50,000 tokens
		Relevant information = 500 tokens

		Without retrieval:

		50,000 tokens → LLM

		With chunking + retrieval:

		500–2,000 relevant tokens → LLM

		This can reduce unnecessary processing and cost, depending on the model and architecture.
		
		
		
	6. Improve Search Precision :

		Imagine a document contains:

		Chunk 1 → Employee Leave
		Chunk 2 → Employee Salary
		Chunk 3 → Employee Benefits
		Chunk 4 → Employee Attendance
		Chunk 5 → Employee Travel

		Question:

		"What is the employee's annual leave entitlement?"

		A vector database can search these chunks and identify:

		Chunk 1 → HIGH similarity
		Chunk 2 → LOW similarity
		Chunk 3 → LOW similarity
		Chunk 4 → LOW similarity
		Chunk 5 → LOW similarity

		So the relevant information is easier to retrieve.
		
		
	7. Preserve Local Context :

		Good chunking doesn't just make text smaller.

		It tries to keep related information together.

		For example:

		Chunk 1
		────────────────────────
		Annual Leave Policy


		Employees receive 18 days
		of annual leave per year.


		Employees must request leave
		through the HR portal.
		────────────────────────

		This is better than splitting every sentence randomly.
		
		
03. Chunk Size :

	What is Chunk Size?

	Chunk size defines how much text should be included in each chunk when a document is split.

	For example:

	Document
	   ↓
	Chunking
	   ↓
	┌──────────────┐
	│ Chunk 1      │ ← 500 units
	├──────────────┤
	│ Chunk 2      │ ← 500 units
	├──────────────┤
	│ Chunk 3      │ ← 500 units
	└──────────────┘

	The important question is:

	500 what? Characters or tokens?
	
	That depends on the chunking method and length_function.
	
	
	1. Simple Example :

		Suppose we have:

		Employee Leave Policy


		Employees are entitled to 18 days of annual leave.
		Employees must submit leave requests through the HR portal.
		Managers are responsible for approving leave requests.
		Employees can carry forward unused leave according to company policy.

		If:

		chunk_size = 100

		the text is divided into smaller pieces around that size.

		If:

		chunk_size = 1000

		the chunks will contain much more information.
		
		
	2. Small Chunk vs Large Chunk :
	
		Small Chunk:
		
		chunk_size = 200

		Conceptually:

		Document
		   │
		   ├── Chunk 1 → 200
		   ├── Chunk 2 → 200
		   ├── Chunk 3 → 200
		   ├── Chunk 4 → 200
		   └── ...
		   
		Advantages: 
		
			More precise retrieval
			Less irrelevant information
			Good for short factual questions
			More focused embeddings
			
		Disadvantages :
		
			Can lose context
			Sentences or concepts may be separated
			More chunks are created
			More vectors need to be stored

	
	3. Large Chunk :

		Example:

		chunk_size = 1500

		Conceptually:

		Document
		   │
		   ├── Chunk 1 → 1500
		   ├── Chunk 2 → 1500
		   ├── Chunk 3 → 1500
		   └── ...
		   
		Advantages :
		
			More context per chunk
			Related information is more likely to stay together
			Useful for complex questions
			Fewer vectors
			
		Disadvantages :
		
			Retrieval may be less precise
			More irrelevant information can be retrieved
			More tokens may be sent to the LLM
			Embeddings represent more mixed information
			
			
	4. Chunk Size vs Chunk Quality :

		A common beginner mistake is:

			"I'll just use 500 characters because everyone uses 500."

			That is not a good approach.

			Instead, think:

				      Chunk Size
					  │
			       ┌──────────┴──────────┐
			          ▼                               ▼
			   Too Small              Too Large
			       │                     │
			          ▼                               ▼
			Lost context          Too much noise
			       │                     │
			       └──────────┬──────────┘
					   ▼
				    Poor Retrieval

			The goal is:

				     Optimal Chunk
					  │
				┌─────────┴─────────┐
				▼                           ▼
			 Enough context       Minimal noise
				│                   │
				└─────────┬─────────┘
					  ▼
				  Better Retrieval
				  
				  
				  
04. Chunk Overlap : 

		What is Chunk Overlap?

		Chunk overlap is the amount of text shared between two consecutive chunks.

		It is used to prevent important information from being lost when a document is split at a boundary.

		Simple example

		Suppose:

		chunk_size = 500
		chunk_overlap = 100

		Then:

		Chunk 1
		├──────────────────────────────────────┤
		1                                    500


				    ↓ 100 characters overlap


		Chunk 2
				    ├──────────────────────────────────────┤
				  401                                    900
				  
				  
				  
				  
		1. Why Do We Need Chunk Overlap?

			Consider this sentence:

			Employees are eligible for annual leave after completing
			six months of continuous service with the organization.

			Imagine the chunk boundary occurs here:
			
			Without overlap:

			Chunk 1:
			Employees are eligible for annual leave after completing


			Chunk 2:
			six months of continuous service with the organization.

			The meaning is split across two chunks.

			This can make retrieval less effective.

			With overlap:

			Chunk 1:
			Employees are eligible for annual leave after completing
			six months of continuous service.


			Chunk 2:
			six months of continuous service. Employees can carry
			forward unused leave according to company policy.

			The important context appears in both chunks.

			So approximately 100 characters from Chunk 1 are repeated in Chunk 2.
			
			
			
		2. Important Trade-Off

			Think of overlap as a balance:

				      Chunk Overlap
					   │
			       ┌───────────┴───────────┐
			          ▼                                  ▼
			Too Little                 Too Much
			       │                       │
			Lost context              Duplication
			       │                       │
			Poor retrieval            More vectors
			       │                       │
			       └───────────┬───────────┘
					    ▼
				      Optimal Range
					   │
					    ▼
				  Better Context Preservation

			The objective is:

			Enough overlap to preserve context, but not so much that you create unnecessary duplication.
			
			Simple rule to start: try 10–20% overlap, then evaluate retrieval quality rather than assuming one value is always best.


05. Characters vs Tokens : 

	This is an important concept in chunking and RAG because chunk_size=500 can mean very different things depending on whether your splitter measures characters or tokens.

	1. What is a Character?

		A character is an individual symbol in text.

		For example:

		Hello

		contains:

		H → character
		e → character
		l → character
		l → character
		o → character

		So:

		"Hello" = 5 characters

		Spaces and punctuation are also characters:

			Hello!

			approximately contains:

			H e l l o ! 
			
			
	2. What is a Token?

		A token is a unit produced by a model's tokenizer.

		A token can be:

			a whole word
			part of a word
			punctuation
			whitespace-related text
			a subword

		For example, conceptually:

		Artificial intelligence is powerful.

		might become something like:

		Artificial
		intelligence
		is
		power
		ful
		.

		The exact tokenization depends on the tokenizer/model.

		So:

		A token is not the same thing as a word or a character.
		
		
	3. Character vs Token

		Consider:

		Artificial Intelligence

		Character-based measurement counts the actual characters:

		A r t i f i c i a l _ I n t e l l i g e n c e

		Token-based measurement uses the model tokenizer.

		Therefore:

		Characters ≠ Tokens

		This is extremely important when designing a RAG system.
		
06. CONTEXT WINDOW :

	1. Definition

		- A Context Window is the maximum amount of tokenized information an LLM can process within a single request/conversation context.

		- In simple words:

			- Context Window = The total token space available to the LLM for understanding instructions, questions, retrieved documents, conversation history, and generating a response.

		Example:

			Context Window = 8,000 tokens

			This means the model has a limited token capacity that must accommodate the information being processed.
			
			
	2. Concept

		- Think of the Context Window like a working desk.
		- The model can only work with the information that fits within its available context.
		
		
		                 LLM
				  │
				   ▼
			┌─────────────────────┐
			│    WORKING DESK     │
			│   8,000 TOKENS      │
			│                     │
			│ System Prompt       │
			│ User Question       │
			│ RAG Chunks          │
			│ Conversation        │
			│ Other Context       │
			│                     │
			└─────────────────────┘
				  │
				   ▼
				Answer
				   
	3. Context Window in Tokens

		- Context windows are measured in tokens, not characters.

			For example:

				Context Window = 8,000 tokens

				The flow is:

					Text
					  │
					  ▼
					Tokenizer
					  │
					  ▼
					Tokens
					  │
					  ▼
					Context Window
					  │
					  ▼
					LLM

				Therefore:

					Characters
					    ↓
					Tokens
					    ↓
					Context Window
							
							
	4. What Goes Inside a Context Window?

		A request can contain:

			CONTEXT WINDOW
			│
			├── System Instructions
			│
			├── User Question
			│
			├── Retrieved RAG Chunks
			│
			├── Conversation History
			│
			├── Tool Results
			│
			└── Other Input

		For example:

			| Company       | Model             |      Context Window | Max Output |
			| ------------- | ----------------- | ------------------: | ---------: |
			| **OpenAI**    | GPT-5.5           |       **1,050,000** |    128,000 |
			| **OpenAI**    | GPT-5.5 Pro       |       **1,050,000** |    128,000 |
			| **Anthropic** | Claude Opus 5     |       **1,000,000** |    128,000 |
			| **Anthropic** | Claude Sonnet 5   |       **1,000,000** |    128,000 |
			| **Anthropic** | Claude Opus 4.7   |       **1,000,000** |    128,000 |
			| **Anthropic** | Claude Opus 4.6   |       **1,000,000** |    128,000 |
			| **Anthropic** | Claude Sonnet 4.6 |       **1,000,000** |     64,000 |
			| **Anthropic** | Claude Haiku 4.5  |         **200,000** |     64,000 |
			| **Google**    | Gemini 1.5 Pro    | **up to 2,000,000** |          — |
			| **Google**    | Gemini 1.5 Flash  |       **1,000,000** |          — |
			
		
		Example :
		
		
			Model:
				GPT-5.5

				Maximum Context Window:
				1,050,000 tokens

				Maximum Output:
				128,000 tokens

			Imagine an LLM has:

				SYSTEM PROMPT          =    5,000 tokens
				USER PROMPT            =    2,000 tokens
				CHAT HISTORY           =   10,000 tokens
				RETRIEVED RAG CHUNKS   =  900,000 tokens
				OTHER CONTEXT          =    5,000 tokens
							 ───────────────
				INPUT TOTAL            =  922,000 tokens
				
			Maximum Context :
					Maximum Context  = 1,050,000

					Used Input =   922,000

					Remaining = 1,050,000 - 922,000 = 128,000 tokens

				There is therefore 128,000 tokens of theoretical remaining capacity, but the actual output limit depends on the model/API and its input/output constraints.
				
				
07 Maximum Token Limit :

	- Maximum Token Limit means the maximum number of tokens an LLM can process within one context window for a single request/conversation turn.
	- It is a hard boundary: if the required input and output exceed the model's supported limit, the request must be shortened, truncated, summarized, or split. 

	Simple Definition :

		- Maximum Token Limit = Maximum number of tokens the model can handle at one time.
		- A token can be a complete word, part of a word, punctuation, or another tokenized unit.
		- The exact number of characters or words represented by a token depends on the tokenizer and language.
		
		
		*****Total Request Tokens ≤ Model Context Window
		

08. Minimum / Maximum Chunk Size

	1. Definition

		- Minimum / Maximum Chunk Size means defining the smallest and largest amount of text allowed in an individual chunk during document chunking.
		
		
		Document
		   │
		    ▼
		Chunking
		   │
		   ├── Minimum Chunk Size
		   │       ↓
		   │   Smallest acceptable chunk
		   │
		   └── Maximum Chunk Size
			   ↓
		       Largest acceptable chunk
		       
	Minimum Chunk Size = Don't create chunks smaller than this limit unless necessary.

	Maximum Chunk Size = Don't allow a chunk to exceed this limit.
	
	
	
	Without limits:

		Document
		   │
		   ├── Chunk 1 → 5 tokens
		   ├── Chunk 2 → 3 tokens
		   ├── Chunk 3 → 800 tokens
		   ├── Chunk 4 → 2,000 tokens
		   └── Chunk 5 → 7 tokens

		This creates poor retrieval quality.

	with limits:

		Document
		   │
		   ▼
		Chunking Rules
		   │
		   ├── Minimum = 100 tokens
		   └── Maximum = 500 tokens
		   │
		   ▼
		Meaningful Chunks
		
09 Top-K :

	1. Definition

		- Top-K means the number of most relevant chunks/documents returned from the vector database for a user's query.
		
	Simple Example : 

		Suppose your PDF has 10 chunks:

		Chunk 1 → Similarity = 0.41
		Chunk 2 → Similarity = 0.92
		Chunk 3 → Similarity = 0.73
		Chunk 4 → Similarity = 0.88
		Chunk 5 → Similarity = 0.32
		Chunk 6 → Similarity = 0.95
		Chunk 7 → Similarity = 0.51
		Chunk 8 → Similarity = 0.81
		Chunk 9 → Similarity = 0.67
		Chunk 10 → Similarity = 0.29

		If:

			top_k = 5

		the system returns:

			Rank    Chunk       Similarity
			───────────────────────────────
			 1      Chunk 6       0.95
			 2      Chunk 2       0.92
			 3      Chunk 4       0.88
			 4      Chunk 8       0.81
			 5      Chunk 3       0.73
			 
			 
10. Fixed-Size Chunking : 

	1. Definition

		Fixed-Size Chunking divides a document into chunks of approximately the same predefined size.

		For example:

		chunk_size = 500 tokens
		
		
		
	Advantages:
	
		✓ Very simple
		✓ Easy to implement
		✓ Fast
		✓ Predictable chunk sizes
		✓ Easy to scale
		✓ Easy to tune
		✓ Good baseline for RAG
		✓ Useful for large plain-text documents

	Disadvantages :
			
		can not split sentences
		can not split paragraphs
		can not split tables
		can not split important concepts
		Doesn't understand document meaning
		May not separate question and answer
		May not separate heading from its content
		
		
	When Should You Use It?

		Use fixed-size chunking when:

			✓ Document is mostly plain text
			✓ Structure is not important
			✓ You need a simple baseline
			✓ You need predictable chunk sizes
			✓ You want fast preprocessing
			✓ You are experimenting with RAG

		Avoid relying on it alone when the document contains:

			✗ Complex tables
			✗ Legal clauses
			✗ Structured forms
			✗ Important headings
			✗ Code
			✗ Complex PDFs
			✗ Layout-dependent information
			
			
10. Character-Based Chunking : 

	1. Definition

		Character-Based Chunking divides text into chunks according to a specified number of characters.

		For example:

		chunk_size = 500

		means:

		≈ 500 characters per chunk

		It is different from token-based chunking because the limit is based on characters, not tokens.
		
		
11. Token-Based Chunking :

	1. Definition

		Token-Based Chunking divides a document according to a specified number of tokens, instead of characters.

		Document
		   │
		   ▼
		Tokenizer
		   │
		   ▼
		Tokens
		   │
		   ▼
		Token-Based Chunking
		   │
		   ├── Chunk 1 → 500 tokens
		   ├── Chunk 2 → 500 tokens
		   ├── Chunk 3 → 500 tokens
		   └── Chunk 4 → remaining tokens

		This is especially useful for LLM/RAG systems because LLM context windows and output limits are measured in tokens.
		
		
		
	Advantages :
			
		✓ Directly controls token count
		✓ Better aligned with LLM context windows
		✓ Better context-budget planning
		✓ Predictable token-based chunk sizes
		✓ Useful for RAG
		✓ Useful for long documents
		✓ Helps control Top-K context
		
	Disadvantages :
	
		✗ Requires tokenizer
		✗ Different models use different tokenizers
		✗ Can split sentences
		✗ Can split paragraphs
		✗ Can split semantic units
		✗ Tokenization adds processing overhead
		✗ Token boundaries don't necessarily equal semantic boundaries
		
		
	When Should You Use It?

		Use token-based chunking when:

			✓ Building RAG
			✓ Working with LLMs
			✓ Context-window management matters
			✓ Token budget matters
			✓ Long documents
			✓ Need predictable token limits
			✓ Testing different chunk sizes
								

12. Sentence-Based Chunking :

	1. Definition

		- Sentence-Based Chunking divides a document into chunks using sentences as the primary boundary.

		- Instead of cutting the document after an arbitrary number of characters or tokens, the system tries to keep complete sentences together.
		
			DOCUMENT
			   │
			    ▼
			Sentence Detection
			   │
			   ├── Sentence 1
			   ├── Sentence 2
			   ├── Sentence 3
			   ├── Sentence 4
			   └── Sentence 5
			   │
			    ▼
			Group Sentences
			   │
			   ├── Chunk 1
			   ├── Chunk 2
			   └── Chunk 3
		
		- Sentence-Based Chunking = Split text at sentence boundaries and group sentences into meaningful chunks.
		
		
		Advantages:
		
			✓ Preserves complete sentences
			✓ Better semantic coherence
			✓ Easy to understand
			✓ Better than raw character splitting
			✓ Useful for natural-language documents
			✓ Good for RAG
			✓ Works well for FAQs and policies
			✓ Easy to combine with token limits
			
		Disadvantages :

			✗ Sentence detection isn't perfect
			✗ Very long sentences can create huge chunks
			✗ Short sentences can create tiny chunks
			✗ Tables don't behave like normal sentences
			✗ Code doesn't behave like normal sentences
			✗ OCR errors can confuse sentence detection
			✗ Some PDFs have poor punctuation
			
		When to Use : 

			Sentence-based chunking is a good choice for:

				✓ HR policies
				✓ FAQs
				✓ Articles
				✓ Emails
				✓ Reports
				✓ General business documents
				✓ Knowledge-base articles
				✓ Natural-language documents

			Less suitable by itself for:

				✗ Tables
				✗ JSON
				✗ XML
				✗ Source code
				✗ Forms
				✗ Highly layout-dependent PDFs
									
									
13. Paragraph-Based Chunking :

	1. Definition

		Paragraph-Based Chunking divides a document using paragraph boundaries as the primary chunking unit.

		Instead of splitting every fixed number of characters or tokens, the system tries to keep the complete paragraph together.

		DOCUMENT
		   │
		    ▼
		Paragraph Detection
		   │
		   ├── Paragraph 1
		   ├── Paragraph 2
		   ├── Paragraph 3
		   └── Paragraph 4
		   │
		    ▼
		Paragraph Chunks
		Simple Definition

		Paragraph-Based Chunking = one or more complete paragraphs are grouped together to create a chunk.	
		
		
		
		
		Advantages:
		
			✓ Preserves complete paragraphs
			✓ Usually preserves one idea
			✓ Better semantic coherence than raw character splitting
			✓ Simple to implement
			✓ Easy to understand
			✓ Good for reports and policies
			✓ Useful for natural-language documents
			✓ Good baseline for RAG
			
		Disadvantages :
		
			✗ Paragraphs can be very large
			✗ Paragraphs can be very small
			✗ Poor formatting can break detection
			✗ OCR can destroy paragraph boundaries
			✗ Tables are not paragraphs
			✗ Forms are not paragraphs
			✗ Long paragraphs may exceed token limits	
			
			
		When Should You Use It :

			Good for :

				✓ Articles
				✓ HR policies
				✓ Reports
				✓ Documentation
				✓ Emails
				✓ Business documents
				✓ Knowledge bases
				✓ Normal text PDFs

			Less suitable alone for :

				✗ Tables
				✗ JSON
				✗ XML
				✗ Source code
				✗ Forms
				✗ Highly structured documents
				✗ Poor-quality OCR	
				
				
				
14. Line-Based Chunking : 

	1. Definition

		Line-Based Chunking splits a document using line boundaries (\n).

		Each line can become an individual chunk, or multiple consecutive lines can be grouped into one chunk.

		DOCUMENT
		   │
		    ▼
		Detect Lines
		   │
		   ├── Line 1
		   ├── Line 2
		   ├── Line 3
		   ├── Line 4
		   └── Line 5
		   │
		    ▼
		Group Lines
		   │
		   ├── Chunk 1
		   ├── Chunk 2
		   └── Chunk 3

		Simple definition:

		Line-Based Chunking = Split text based on newline boundaries.	
		
		
		
		Advantages :
		
			✓ Very simple
			✓ Preserves line boundaries
			✓ Useful for OCR text
			✓ Useful for logs
			✓ Useful for invoices
			✓ Useful for packing slips
			✓ Useful for receipts
			✓ Easy to implement overlap

		Disadvantages :

			✗ A line may not contain a complete idea
			✗ PDF/OCR line breaks may be incorrect
			✗ One sentence may span multiple lines
			✗ Lines can have very different lengths
			✗ Not inherently token-aware
			✗ Poor OCR can create many meaningless lines	
			
			
			
15. Page-Based Chunking :

		1. Definition

			Page-Based Chunking divides a document according to its original page boundaries.

			For a PDF:

				PDF
				 │
				 ├── Page 1 → Chunk 1
				 ├── Page 2 → Chunk 2
				 ├── Page 3 → Chunk 3
				 └── Page 4 → Chunk 4

				The important difference from the previous methods is:

				Line-Based
				    ↓
				Line boundary

				Paragraph-Based
				    ↓
				Paragraph boundary

				Page-Based
				    ↓
				PDF page boundary
